Land & Geometric Shape Formulas (Definitions & Applications)

Select a shape category below to view formal definitions, practical land-surveying applications, side-based formulas, and angle-based formulas.

1. Triangle — Heron's Formula (Three Sides Known)

Definition: A closed three-sided polygon determined uniquely by the lengths of its three sides a, b, c.
Applications: Primary method in chain/tape land surveying (Gunter's chain or steel tape) where irregular fields are subdivided into triangles and only linear distances are measured without angular instruments.
s = a + b + c2   |   Area = √s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)
where a, b, c = triangle side lengths, and s = semi-perimeter.

2. Triangle — Kahan's Formula (Numerically Stable Three-Side Formula)

Definition: A rearranged, floating-point-safe formulation of Heron's formula introduced by numerical analyst William Kahan.
Applications: Essential when surveying narrow boundary strips, road-widening slivers, or acute wedge-shaped plots where one side is nearly equal to the sum of the other two (preventing severe rounding loss in standard Heron's formula).
Sort sides so that  x ≥ y ≥ z
Area = 14 √(x + (y + z)) · (z − (x − y)) · (z + (x − y)) · (x + (y − z))
Evaluate inner parentheses strictly in the order shown.

3. Triangle — Angle-Based (SAS) & Base-Altitude Formulas

Definition: Area expressed via two adjacent sides a, b and their included interior angle θ, or via a baseline and perpendicular offset height h.
Applications: Used in total-station/theodolite surveys (SAS) and traditional cross-staff / optical-square offset surveys along a main survey chain line (Base × Height).
SAS Formula:   Area = 12 a · b · sin(θ)   |   Base–Height:   Area = 12 × b × h

4. Irregular Quadrilateral (General Four-Sided Plot)

Definition: A four-sided polygon with unequal sides a, b, c, d and no mandatory parallel sides or right angles. Unlike a triangle, four side lengths alone do not fix its shape; one diagonal or two opposite angles are required.
Applications: The most common shape of rural agricultural fields and residential plots in J&K revenue records. Measured either by running one diagonal tie-line p across opposite corners or by recording opposite corner angles with a compass/theodolite.
Method A — Diagonal Split (Diagonal p):
Area = Area(Δ1: a, b, p) + Area(Δ2: c, d, p)

Method B — Bretschneider's Formula (Opposite Angles α, γ):
s = a + b + c + d2,   θ = α + γ2
Area = √(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)(s − d) − a·b·c·d · cos2(θ)

Method C — Two Diagonals (d1, d2) & Intersecting Angle (φ):
Area = 12 d1 · d2 · sin(φ)

5. Square

Definition: A regular quadrilateral with four equal sides of length a and four 90° right angles. Its two diagonals are equal (d = a√2) and bisect each other perpendicularly.
Applications: Standard unit grid blocks (such as 1 Karam × 1 Karam = 1 Sarsahi in J&K land measurement, where 9 Sarsahi = 1 Marla), square building foundations, and urban housing plots.
From Side (a):   Area = a2   |   Perimeter = 4a
From Diagonal (d):   Area = d22   |   Diagonal d = a√2

6. Rectangle

Definition: A four-sided polygon with four 90° right angles in which opposite sides are equal in length (Length L and Width W).
Applications: Planned residential colony plots, commercial shop sites, rectangular orchard blocks, and room/floor area calculations. Diagonal check d = √(L2 + W2) verifies true 90° corners on site.
Area:   Area = L × W   |   Perimeter = 2(L + W)
Diagonal (d):   d = √L2 + W2   |   Area from L & d = L √d2 − L2

7. Rhombus

Definition: An equilateral quadrilateral whose four sides all have equal length a, opposite angles are equal, and whose diagonals d1, d2 bisect each other at 90° right angles.
Applications: Skewed diamond-shaped plots at oblique road intersections where all four boundary walls are equal in length but corners are not 90°.
From Diagonals (d1, d2):   Area = 12 d1 × d2
From Side (a) & Interior Angle (θ):   Area = a2 · sin(θ)
From Side (a) & Perpendicular Height (h):   Area = a × h

8. Trapezium (Trapezoid)

Definition: A quadrilateral having at least one pair of parallel opposite sides (called bases a and b) separated by perpendicular distance h, with non-parallel legs c and d.
Applications: Very common in plots bounded by a straight road or canal on one side and a parallel boundary wall on the opposite side, while the side boundaries taper inward or outward. Also used in the Trapezoidal Rule for irregular river/stream boundaries.
Parallel Bases (a, b) & Perpendicular Height (h):
Area = a + b2 × h

All Four Sides Known (a ∥ b, a ≠ b):
k = |a − b|,   st = k + c + d2
Area = a + bk √st(st − k)(st − c)(st − d)

Angle-Based (Bases a, b, Leg c, Base Angle θ):
Area = a + b2 · c · sin(θ)

9. Parallelogram

Definition: A four-sided polygon with two pairs of parallel and equal opposite sides (a and b), where opposite interior angles are equal.
Applications: Terraced hill-slope plots or canal-side strips where opposite boundaries run parallel at a slant angle rather than 90°.
Base & Perpendicular Height:   Area = b × h
Adjacent Sides (a, b) & Included Angle (θ):   Area = a · b · sin(θ)
Diagonals (d1, d2) & Angle Between Diagonals (φ):   Area = 12 d1 · d2 · sin(φ)

10. Kite & Cyclic Quadrilateral (Brahmagupta's Formula)

Definition: A Kite has two pairs of equal-length adjacent sides (a = d and b = c) with perpendicular diagonals. A Cyclic Quadrilateral is any four-sided polygon whose vertices lie on a single circle (opposite angles sum to 180°).
Applications: Symmetrical corner plots (kite) and maximum-area four-sided enclosures for given perimeter lengths (cyclic quadrilateral).
Kite (Diagonals d1, d2):   Area = 12 d1 × d2   |   Angle-Based: Area = a · b · sin(θ)
Cyclic Quadrilateral (Brahmagupta's Formula):   Area = √(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)(s − d)

11. Circle & Circular Sector

Definition: A locus of points equidistant (radius r) from a center point; a sector is a wedge bounded by two radii and an arc of central angle θ.
Applications: Circular wells, water storage tanks, roundabouts, and curved road-bend plot boundaries.
Full Circle:   Area = π r2   |   Circumference = 2 π r
Circular Sector (θ in degrees):   Area = θ360° × π r2

How to Use This Application — Step-by-Step Guide

Step 1 — Draw the Field Boundary:
Open 1. Field Sketch and tap Start / Redraw Field. Tap/click around the outer corners of your land plot in order to place boundary points P1, P2, P3, P4…. Use Undo Point at the top-right of the canvas if you misplace a corner, then press Finish Field once all outer corners are marked.
Step 2 — Divide the Field into Triangles:
Tap any three vertices to form a triangle. You can select existing boundary points (P1, P2…), reuse previously created internal points (A1, A2…), or tap anywhere inside/on the field boundary to place a new vertex (A1, A2…). Points outside the polygon are blocked automatically. Press Add Triangle at the top-right of the sketch. Once triangles cover 100% of the polygon, Add Triangle locks automatically.
Step 3 — Enter Measured Side Lengths (in Feet):
In Section 3 (Triangle Measurement Table), enter the actual ground-measured length in feet for each side (B1, B2… for boundary edges and D1, D2… for internal diagonals/chords). Any shared side used by two or more triangles retains a single Side ID and auto-fills everywhere. Both Heron's Area and Kahan's Area compute instantly.
Step 4 — Adjust Shape to True Scale & Zoom Triangles:
After entering all triangle side lengths, click Submit Sides & Adjust Shape. The sketch reshapes proportionally to match your actual field measurements, color-coding every triangle and displaying its ID and area clearly. Tap any triangle on the sketch (or use the zoom buttons below the canvas) to zoom into a single triangle or return to Full Field View.
Step 5 — Manage Multiple Sketches & Export Reports:
Tap Next Sketch (+ Save Current) to store the active plot and start a new sketch; switch between saved plots anytime using the Saved Plots bar. Use the export buttons in Section 5 to save your survey as a PDF report, a high-resolution JPG sketch image, or a CSV / Excel (.XLS) spreadsheet.
Step 6 — Standalone Triangle Calculator & Shape Formulas:
Use 2. Triangle Calc to compute any single triangle directly from three sides using both Heron's and Kahan's formulas, and browse 3. Shape Formulas for definitions, applications, and formulas for irregular quadrilaterals, squares, rectangles, rhombuses, trapeziums, and parallelograms.
